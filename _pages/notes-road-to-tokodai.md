Some friends asked me how the process for getting an academic position in Japan looks like, and one of them suggested me to just write it down. So here it is. I try to write it honestly, including the parts where I was not so sure about myself. Hopefully this can be useful for someone who is in the same position as me before, especially for international students who finished their Ph.D. in Japan and are thinking about academia.

Just for context, "Tokodai" (東工大) is how many of us still call Tokyo Institute of Technology. Since October 2024 it merged with Tokyo Medical and Dental University and became the Institute of Science Tokyo (Science Tokyo). I did my M.Eng. and Ph.D. there, in the Department of Systems and Control Engineering. So in a way, this is a story about going back home.

## Where I was before this

I finished my Ph.D. in September 2025. My research was about coordinated multi-drone image sampling for 3D map reconstruction, basically how a team of drones can decide where and how to take pictures while the 3D map is still being built, and using the map itself as a feedback signal.

After graduating, I joined [DeepX](https://deepx.co.jp/) in November 2025 as a Robotics Control Engineer, working on coordinated planning for multiple autonomous excavators. Honestly I really enjoy working in industry. Seeing the algorithm running on a real heavy machine is a different kind of happiness. But in the back of my mind, I always wanted to teach and to build my own research group someday. So when the opening came, I decided to try.

## Finding the opening

The position was announced on [JREC-IN Portal](https://jrecin.jst.go.jp/), which is the main job portal for research positions in Japan, run by JST. Most of university positions in Japan are posted there, and many of them also have an English version. The call for this one opened around the beginning of April 2026, with the deadline at the end of June 2026.

Some small tips about JREC-IN:

- Make an account and save search conditions (field, position, location), so you can get the new postings by email.
- Read the posting very carefully. Each university and each department has their own required documents and format, and sometimes they have a designated form that you must use.
- Many universities also have their own job page. For Science Tokyo, you can also check the [official job advertisement page](https://www.jinji2.jim.titech.ac.jp/koubo/en/).

## Preparing the application documents

For this position, the application was one combined document. The structure I submitted was like this:

1. **Curriculum Vitae (designated form).** The university provided the form. Education, work experience with job description, degrees, memberships, and honors. One small thing: the dates need year, month, and day, so I had to dig my old documents to get the exact dates.
2. **Accomplishment statement.** Full list of journal papers, conference papers, book chapter, patents, theses, and citation metrics from Google Scholar (with the access date).
3. **Major publications (up to three).** The full papers themselves are attached. I chose the ones most relevant to the position.
4. **Research grants and external funds.** For me, mostly scholarships during my graduate study.
5. **Research statement.** Summary of what I have done, and the plan for the future.
6. **Teaching statement.** Summary of teaching experience, and the teaching plan.
7. **Community engagement record.** Memberships, reviewer and session chair activities, invited talks, and community activities.
8. **References.** Two people who can be contacted.

Here are the three papers I chose as my major publications, in case you are curious about the kind of work I submitted:

- *Coverage-Recon: Coordinated Multi-Drone Image Sampling with Online Map Feedback*, under review at IEEE Transactions on Control Systems Technology. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18347) · [project page](https://htnk-lab.github.io/coverage-recon/)
- *Efficient Angle-Aware Coverage Control for Large-Scale Map Reconstruction using Drone Networks*, SICE JCMSI, 2024. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/18824889.2024.2346375)
- *Angle-Aware Coverage with Camera Rotational Motion Control*, SICE JCMSI, 2024. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/18824889.2024.2351637)

Some things I learned while writing them:

- **Tell one story, not a list.** At first my research statement was just a list of projects. Then I tried to find the one question that connects all of them: how can multiple robots work together safely and efficiently in the real world. After that, everything became easier to write, and it also helped me later in the interview.
- **The future plan should grow from what you already did.** I tried to make the plan feel like a natural continuation, not a totally new topic. For me it was about closed-loop autonomy, where perception, planning, and control are connected through feedback from the environment.
- **Teaching experience is more than TA.** I was worried that I don't have much "official" teaching experience. But when I wrote it down, there were more than I thought: tutoring math olympiad for kids when I was in high school, TA for several courses, giving seminars for Indonesian students about graduate study and scholarships, and organizing educational activities for Indonesian kids in Japan. All of these count.
- **Industry experience is a plus, not a gap.** I was a bit worried that being in a company would look like I "left" academia. But I wrote it as a strength: working with real machines and real constraints made my view about research more practical.
- **Ask your references early.** Contact them weeks before the deadline, explain the position, and send them your CV. Don't make them rush.
- **Check the small things.** Photo requirements, dates format, page numbers, file size. It is boring but it matters.

## Passing the document screening

In July 2026 I got an email that my application passed the document screening, and I was invited for an interview. Alhamdulillah, I was really happy reading that email. **[TODO: exact date]**

The interview format was:

- A **20-minute presentation** about myself, my research achievements, and my future research plan after joining Science Tokyo.
- A **Q&A session** with the selection committee.

It was mainly in person at the university, but online was also possible if requested. They gave several time slots and asked us to choose all the slots we were available, so I just picked the ones that didn't clash with my work schedule.

A few things that helped me:

- **Practice with a timer, many times.** 20 minutes goes really fast. I cut many slides that I liked.
- **Prepare backup slides.** For questions that I expect, like more detail about the method, or the plan for getting research funding.
- **Know the department.** Read about the labs and the professors there, and think about where you can collaborate and what you can contribute that is still missing.
- **Be honest in Q&A.** If I didn't know something, I just said it, and explained how I would approach it.

## If you are thinking to apply

To summarize, some things I wish I knew earlier:

- Keep your CV and publication list always updated, so when the opening comes you are not starting from zero.
- Start writing research and teaching statements before there is a position. You can revise it later.
- Your story matters more than the number of papers. Make it clear what is your question and why you are the right person for it.
- Small things like community activities and outreach also count. Write them down.
- And just try. I almost didn't apply because I felt I was not ready yet.

If you are a student or young researcher thinking about this path, especially from Indonesia, feel free to reach out. I'm happy to share more.
